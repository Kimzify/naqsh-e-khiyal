# Design notes

These notes are what the pages learned from reviewing each scene frame by frame. New poems should follow them.

## Meaning

- **Use clear, conceptual symbols from Persian poetry.** They can be a little abstract, but a reader should recognise them and connect them to the couplet, ideally through the poem's own words or their double meanings. For example, «مهر» means both sun and kindness, which became an eclipse, and «صحیفهٔ هستی» became footprints on a shore washed by a wave.
- **Cover every part of the couplet.** In «حال ما در فرقت جانان و ابرام رقیب / جمله می‌داند خدای حال‌گردان», the image has to hold the separation, the rival's pestering *and* the turning of the state: a candle behind glass, a moth thrown back by the wind, and a door that opens by itself. An hourglass only shows "time". A sunflower has no rival.
- **Avoid references that need a footnote.** The Nowruz orange that turns in a bowl of water was accurate, but a reader couldn't work it out.
- **Never show a concept by writing the word.** Don't write «غم», «نیک» or «بد» into a scene. A line written inside a scene must be the complete hemistich, and it must look the way the poem says ("written in gold" means gold).
- **One visual idea across the whole poem** is better than a loose picture for each couplet.
- **Keep endings restrained, not sweet.** If the poem doesn't promise union, don't show it.
- **No religious imagery**, even where the poem names it. That means no Quran pages, rahl, Kaaba or prayer objects.

## Drawing

- **Don't add details that mean nothing.** A coloured sash on a figure confused readers.
- **Draw objects well, in proportion and from a natural angle.** Hand-held objects must actually be held. Nothing should land on or cover a face.
- **Keep people small, in silhouette or seen from a distance.** Close-up figures have failed repeatedly, for example in how a book was held.
- **Be true to the world.** A desert has no trees, a lamp stands upright, and a noria shouldn't look like a Ferris wheel.
- **Let appearances build gradually.** A moon or a light should never pop in. The poem's high point gets the richest scene.

## Sound

- Keep the music mellow and suited to the poem: no drums, and no note on every word.
- Use a different dastgah for each poem, and a recurring figure at the end of each couplet that mirrors the radif.

## Rejected ideas (so they are not tried again)

- A tangled knot for sorrow.
- A gold flower vs a black thorn for good and bad.
- Dice on a nard board.
- A rose made of thorns with a glowing name.
- A cracked mirror of the heart.
- A broken rosary whose beads gather back.
- A shadow play of Joseph's story.
- A waterwheel.
- An hourglass.
- Trees in a desert.
- A flower in a separate space outside a door.
- A frontal reader with a book on the lap.
